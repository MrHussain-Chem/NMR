🧪 NMR Spectroscopy Analysis

A Step-by-Step Guide for A-Level Chemistry Students

¹³C NMR Analysis
1
Count the Peaks - Understanding Symmetry
Each peak = one unique carbon environment (symmetry reduces peaks!)
Symmetry Examples:
Propan-2-ol: CH₃-CH(OH)-CH₃ → 2 peaks (not 3!) • Both CH₃ groups are identical (mirror images) • Only 2 different carbon environments

Butane: CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃ → 2 peaks (not 4!) • End CH₃ groups are equivalent • Middle CH₂ groups are equivalent

2-Methylpropane: (CH₃)₃CH → 2 peaks (not 4!) • All three CH₃ groups are identical • Only CH and CH₃ environments

No Symmetry - Butan-2-ol: CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ → 4 peaks • All carbons in different environments • OH group breaks symmetry
↓
2
Identify Chemical Shifts (AQA Data Sheet)
Use the AQA chemical shift ranges:
Example: Ethyl ethanoate CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ • ~14 ppm: CH₃ (alkyl) • ~21 ppm: CH₃CO (next to C=O) • ~61 ppm: OCH₂ (carbon next to oxygen) • ~171 ppm: C=O (carbonyl carbon)
↓
3
Match to Molecular Formula
Check your structure matches:
Example: C₄H₈O₂ (4 peaks in ¹³C NMR) • 4 carbons ✓ • Look for functional groups based on chemical shifts • Peak at ~170 ppm confirms C=O • Could be butanoic acid or methyl propanoate • Use both NMR spectra together to distinguish
¹H NMR Analysis
1
Count Peaks & Integration
Each peak = one unique H environment
Integration = number of protons in that environment
Example: Ethanol CH₃CH₂OH • 3 peaks in ratio 3:2:1 • CH₃ (3H) : CH₂ (2H) : OH (1H) • Integration shows HOW MANY H are in each environment • OH may not always integrate properly (exchangeable)
↓
2
Analyze Chemical Shifts (AQA Data Sheet)
Identify functional groups using AQA ranges:
Example: Ethanol CH₃CH₂OH • 1.2 ppm: CH₃ (alkyl, triplet) • 3.7 ppm: CH₂ (next to OH, quartet) • 5.4 ppm: OH (exchangeable, broad singlet) Note: OH chemical shift varies with concentration
↓
3
Examine Splitting Patterns
Apply n+1 rule (only for AQA A-level):
Example 1: Ethyl ethanoate CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ • 1.3 ppm: triplet (CH₃, split by 2H on adjacent CH₂) • 2.1 ppm: singlet (CH₃CO, no adjacent H) • 4.1 ppm: quartet (OCH₂, split by 3H on adjacent CH₃)

Example 2: Propan-1-ol CH₃CH₂CH₂OH • 1.0 ppm: triplet (CH₃, split by 2H on adjacent CH₂) • 1.6 ppm: quartet (middle CH₂, split by 3H on adjacent CH₃) • 3.7 ppm: triplet (CH₂OH, split by 2H on adjacent CH₂)

Example 3: Ethanol CH₃CH₂OH • 1.2 ppm: triplet (CH₃, split by 2H on adjacent CH₂) • 3.7 ppm: quartet (CH₂, split by 3H on adjacent CH₃) • OH doesn't follow n+1 rule (exchangeable) Remember: equivalent H don't split each other
↓
4
Build the Structure
Piece together the fragments systematically:
Example: C₃H₆O (aldehyde vs ketone?) • Triplet at 1.1 ppm (3H) + Quartet at 2.4 ppm (2H) = CH₃CH₂- • Singlet at 9.8 ppm (1H) = CHO (aldehyde H) • Structure: CH₃CH₂CHO (propanal) not CH₃COCH₃ Aldehyde H is distinctive at ~9.7 ppm
💡 AQA A-Level NMR Success Tips
Mastering Symmetry in NMR
Key Rule: Carbons (or hydrogens) that can be swapped by rotation/reflection are equivalent

Test for Equivalence:
• Can you rotate the molecule to make the atoms identical?
• Are they mirror images across a plane of symmetry?
• If YES to either = equivalent = same peak

Symmetry Breakers:
• Functional groups (OH, COOH, CHO)
• Different substituents
• Chiral centers (Carbon with 4 different groups/atoms attached to it)
Use Your Data Sheet
The AQA data sheet gives you chemical shift ranges. Don't memorize - learn to use the sheet effectively. Focus on recognizing patterns rather than exact values.
More Symmetry Practice
Propanone (acetone): CH₃COCH₃ → 2 peaks
• Both CH₃ groups equivalent

Ethyl methanoate: HCOOCH₂CH₃ → 3 peaks
• CHO, OCH₂, CH₃ all different

Diethyl ether: CH₃CH₂OCH₂CH₃ → 2 peaks
• Perfect symmetry around oxygen
• Both CH₃ equivalent, both CH₂ equivalent
Classic AQA Patterns
Ethyl group: Triplet (~1.2 ppm) + Quartet (~4.1 ppm if next to O)
Propyl group: Triplet + Quartet + Multiplet
Aromatic: Usually 7.0-7.5 ppm region
Symmetry in ¹H NMR Too!
Propan-2-ol ¹H NMR:
• 6H at ~1.2 ppm (both CH₃ groups, doublet)
• 1H at ~4.0 ppm (CH, septet)
• 1H at ~2.5 ppm (OH, broad)

Key Point: Integration shows 6:1:1 ratio because of equivalent hydrogens!
Integration Ratios & Symmetry
Always simplify ratios: 6:4:2 becomes 3:2:1. Large integrations often indicate symmetry (multiple equivalent H). If integration doesn't match, check for overlapping peaks or exchangeable protons (OH, NH).
Common AQA Mistakes
• Biggest Error: Forgetting about symmetry (always check for equivalent groups!)
• Not recognizing CH₃CO groups (singlet ~2.1 ppm)
• Confusing aldehydes (~9.7 ppm) with aromatics (~7.2 ppm)
• Missing that high integration = multiple equivalent H
Exam Strategy
1. Start with molecular formula: Note the number of carbons, hydrogens, and other atoms
2. Count peaks in both spectra
3. Check for symmetry: Fewer peaks than atoms = symmetry!
4. Look for "giveaway" signals (CHO, COOH, aromatic)
5. Build from the most obvious fragments
6. Use both spectra together - ¹³C shows skeleton, ¹H shows detail
Key AQA Functional Groups & Symmetry Quick Check
¹³C NMR: C=O (~170-200 ppm), Aromatic (~120-140 ppm)
¹H NMR: CHO (~9.7 ppm), COOH (~11-12 ppm), Aromatic (~7.2 ppm)

Quick Symmetry Test: Draw the structure, look for a line/plane of symmetry. Atoms on opposite sides of this line are equivalent!
